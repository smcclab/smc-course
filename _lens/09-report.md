Your **LENS report** is a single document which describes the development of
your computer music ideas and your LENS artefact over the course of the whole
semester. It may contain edited versions of your process blog with additional
sections to describe your intentions and evaluate your LENS artefact and final
performance. The report must be self-contained; it must make sense to a reader
who has _not_ read your process blog.

We are looking for reports that demonstrate a sophisticated understanding of
music computing as well as a critical examination of the process of developing a
LENS artefact. As with any other piece of academic writing we expect a
high-quality document with clarity of expression favoured over quantity of
words.

## Submission

- the report should be a single PDF document, and is due for submission by **9pm
  on Wednesday 6 November**
  
- the report is worth **30% of your grade**

## Structure

The report should have three sections: "intention", "process" and "evaluation".
A rough guide to length is 2000 words: 500 for intention, 1000 for process, and
500 for evaluation (although the exact balance is up to you). There is no page
limit or limit to the number images in your document, but they must be relevant,
with clear explanations either in a caption or referred to by name (e.g. "Figure
3 shows...") in the text.

### Intention

_approximately 500 words_

This section introduces _your_ goals for your LENS artefact design. This should
include excerpts (revised & made more focussed) from your design rationale. As
usual, we want to know _why_ you chose your specific design goals, and _how_
they relate to the learning outcomes. Make sure to include how your goals relate
to digital synthesis, algorithmic composition, musical interface design, and
collaborative performance.

### Process

_approximately 1000 words_

You should edit each of your process blog entries down to a maximum of 200
words. The entries should be edited to focus on the evolution of your LENS
design ideas and implementations. Good things to include would be: how learning
about a computer music technique changed your approach to creating your
artefact, or how the open rehearsal clarified your goals or helped you
understand the performance issues related to LENS artefacts. Don't try and cover
too much ground in each one---pick one key idea (for each stage) and make that
point clearly.

### Evaluation

_approximately 500 words_

Your evaluation should discuss how you met the design goals set out in the
intention section. You should also discuss any setbacks in creating your LENS
artefact, why they occurred and how you overcame them or worked around them. The
evaluation should also reflect on the experience of your final LENS performance.
Assess how your LENS artefact worked (or didn't) in the performance, and how
successful the collaborative elements were. Finally, reflect critically on your
experiences during LENS to discuss future ideas for your artefact and what you
might do next.

## Marking criteria

Your report will be assessed entirely based on the provided (pdf) document. The
marking criteria (based on the [course learning outcomes]({{site.baseurl}}{%
link _lens/01-outline.md %})) are:

- understanding of **fundamental aspects of music computing** including digital
  synthesis, algorithmic composition and musical interface design

- understanding of **design and performance issues** around computer music
  interfaces

- understanding of **collaborative performance** with computer music interfaces

- **critical examination and self-reflection** on your LENS artefact and
  performances throughout the development process

- **presentation and communication** of your intention, process, and evaluation
  through your LENS report

## Some hints on putting together your document

- **Clarity is the key**: you need to explain where you started, where you ended
  up, and what you learned along the way. It's not a bedtime story, but it
  should have a narrative arc.

- **Critical examination** of your process and performances is just as important
  as producing an excellent LENS artefact.

- **Do** look again at the [course outline]({{site.baseurl}}{% link
  _lens/01-outline.md %}). The learning outcomes are how we assess your work.

- **Do** make sure you examine your decisions and the outcomes carefully, ask
  why again ([see five whys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Five_Whys)).

- **Do** use headings to structure your work.

- **Do** use figures, but make sure they are relevant to and referenced in the
  text.

- Finally, **don't** leave this to the last minute, quality writing takes time.
  Charles can give you feedback on your report after Week 11.
