<!-- This paper contrasts two perspectives on encouraging the development of an electronic and experimental music scene among university students: that of composition students within a music department, and creative coding students within a computer science department. Computer music has a long history of collaboration and cross-disciplinary development from music and computing faculties. In our efforts to foster engagement with music from both music and computing students we have pursued strategies such as free improvisation performances and open work compositions from the music side; and laptop ensembles, and software engineering processes from the computing side. These approaches embrace the commonalities, and acknowledge the differences between students on both sides of this interdisciplinary field, while both producing musical performance practices.  -->

<!-- In this research, we report on these processes and discuss contrasts between both approaches. We address the impact of new trends in both music and computing education, such as a new focus on broad-audience coding education, and music production with the academy. Finally, we evaluate the impacts of these perspectives on the students and examine the lasting computer music practices that have emerged so far. -->
