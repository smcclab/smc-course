## Outline of changes from 2019

### Workshop

There will still be one 2hr in-person scheduled workshop slot, which all
students are expected to attend. However, we won't do the "teach you new stuff
in the workshop thing", instead we'll give them stuff to read, do **and submit**
on their own time, then in the workshop we'll discuss the submissions.

Each week students will:

- learn about a particular computer music concept (_in their own time_)
- make & submit a small creative (music/noise/sound) piece/tool which explores
  that concept (_before the workshop slot_)
- listen to, play with & discuss the things that they and their classmates have
  made (_during the class workshop timeslot_)
  
_p.s.: should we call it a workshop, or a studio session, or a lab, or a
tutorial?_

### Deliverables

I think we ditch the blog; the "ongoing reflection stuff" can be incorporated
into the weekly workshop deliverables, with the added bonus that the students

Assessment breakdown-wise, I think (from the COMP2710 side) we don't even have
to pretend that we're adhering to the "research project" mark breakdown; we can
choose a new breakdown if it suits.

I'd rather not mark the weekly submissions individually, though---perhaps get

## Learning outcomes {#learning-outcomes}

Upon successful completion of this course, students will be able to:

1. Describe & apply **fundamental aspects of music computing** including
   digital synthesis, algorithmic composition, and musical interface design.

2. Demonstrate knowledge and understanding of **design and performance
   issues** around computer music interfaces.

3. **Critically examine** computer music systems and laptop ensemble performances.

4. **Produce** a computer musical instrument, composition or interactive
   media work and **present it in concert**.

5. Demonstrate **collaboration skills** with a laptop ensemble including
   being able to provide **design feedback** and **critical self-reflection**.

## Course deliverables {#course-deliverables}

| Workshop portfolio             | week 5 (wk 1-5) & week 10 (wk 6-10) | 15% + 15% = 30% |

| LENS Performance               | end of week 12                      | 50%             |

### Workshop portfolio (30%)

A document with a short (1/2 page per week) explanation of how each week's
workshop submission satisfied the requirements and explored the week's "concept".

Assessed in 2 parts: weeks 1-5 (due at the end of week 5) and weeks 6-10 (due at
the end of week 10)

Note: we're not marking the video, we're marking their reflection on how the
video demonstrates the LOs.

### Masterclass & performance plan (20%)

In week 7 (first week after the teaching break) you will give a masterclass to
the rest of the LENS cohort describing your plan for your final LENS
performance, and also submit a document describing your LENS performance and how
it will address the assessment criteria above.

Each component (in-class presentation and document) is worth 10%, for a total of
20% of the course grade.

### LENS Performance (50%)

Your LENS performance could be a laptop ensemble composition, a computer musical
instrument, a live visualisation, a networked performance system, a new musical
interface. It must involve live performance, and real-time processing (so a
pre-rendered audio or video file will not be appropriate).

There are many options here and we will help you to choose a LENS performance
that suits your personal goals but challenges you to get outside your comfort
zone whether you are a CS major, artist, musician, or something else!

You are required to submit and will be graded on all software/composition
materials (the _LENS Artefact_) as well as the performance.
